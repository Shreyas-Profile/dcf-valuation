The big idea

What is a DCF?

Discounted Cash Flow works out what a company is worth today, based on the cash it will make in the future.

Three steps
1. Guess future casheach year
2. Shrink it to today's valuediscount
3. Add it all up÷ shares
Resultfair price / share

Key takeaways

  • A company is worth the cash it will make, not the hype around it.
  • The answer is only as good as the guesses you put in.

In the app: every slider changes one of the guesses, and the price updates live.

The input

Free Cash Flow

The cash a company has left over each year after paying its costs, its tax and its big investments.

Example: a company with $100 of sales
Sales (revenue)$100
− Costs−$60
− Tax−$10
− Investment (CapEx)−$10
Free cash flow$20

Key takeaways

  • It's the money that could actually be handed to owners.
  • Faster growth and higher margins mean more free cash flow.

In the app: the 5-year bar chart shows this number for each year.

The key trick

Discounting

Money later is worth less than money now, because money now could be earning interest. Discounting shrinks future money back to today's value.

$100 received in each future year, valued today (at 10%)
100Now
91Y1
83Y2
75Y3
68Y4
62Y5

Key takeaways

  • It's interest running backwards: divide instead of multiply.
  • The further away the money, the less it's worth today.

In the app: the WACC slider sets how hard future cash gets shrunk.

The risk dial

What is WACC?

The discount rate: the yearly return investors want for the risk of owning the company. Riskier company, higher WACC.

$100 arriving next year is worth today…
Safe company (WACC 7%)$93
Risky company (WACC 12%)$89
Full name: Weighted Average Cost of Capital

Key takeaways

  • Higher WACC means a lower fair price.
  • Small changes matter a lot: +2% can cut the value by a quarter.

In the app: the "Rate Spike" button raises WACC to show this.

The long tail

Terminal Growth

We only predict 5 years in detail. After that we assume the company grows slowly and steadily forever, usually 2–3% a year. The value of all those years is the terminal value.

The shortcut formula
Terminal value =
next year's cash ÷ (WACC − growth)
e.g. $91 ÷ (9% − 3%) = $1,520

Key takeaways

  • It's often over 70% of the total value.
  • So the guess about the far future matters most.

In the app: the heatmap tests every mix of WACC and terminal growth.

The verdict

Fair Value vs Price

The price is what people pay for a share today. The fair value is what the DCF says it should cost. The gap is the point.

Market price$200
DCF fair value$250
25% below fair value: looks undervalued

Key takeaways

  • Fair value above price: the share looks cheap.
  • Fair value below price: the share looks expensive.

In the app: the scoreboard shows both numbers and the % gap.

The hidden cost

Stock-Based Pay (SBC)

Many tech companies pay staff partly in new shares. More shares means each one is a smaller slice of the company. This is called dilution.

100 shares become 110. Owning 1 share used to be 1%; now it's 0.91%.

Key takeaways

  • It's a real cost, even though no cash leaves the company.
  • Ignoring it makes a company look more valuable than it is.

In the app: the SBC toggle in Forensic Settings shows its effect.
